## 1. Filing an FIR (First Information Report)

### What is an FIR?
An FIR is a written document prepared by police when they receive information about the commission of a cognizable offence. It is the first step in the criminal justice process.

### Your Rights When Filing an FIR:
- **Police MUST register your FIR** for cognizable offences (Section 154 CrPC / Section 173 BNSS). Refusal is punishable under Section 166A IPC / Section 170 BNS.
- You have the right to receive a **free copy of the FIR** immediately.
- If the officer in charge refuses to register an FIR, you can:
  - Send a written complaint by post to the **Superintendent of Police (SP)**.
  - File a complaint with a **Judicial Magistrate** under Section 156(3) CrPC / Section 175(3) BNSS.
  - File an **online FIR** through the state police website (available in most states).
- A **woman complainant** has the right to have her statement recorded at her residence or a place of her choice.
- A **female officer** must record statements of women victims in sexual offence cases.
- The FIR can be filed in **any language** the complainant is comfortable with.
- If you are **physically unable** to go to the police station, the police must come to you.

### How to File an FIR:
1. Visit the nearest police station (jurisdiction of the incident).
2. Narrate the incident to the Station House Officer (SHO).
3. The SHO writes down the complaint — read it carefully before signing.
4. Collect your copy of the FIR with the FIR number.
5. Note the investigating officer's name and contact details.

## 2. Zero FIR

### What is a Zero FIR?
A Zero FIR can be filed at **ANY police station**, regardless of where the crime occurred. The police station that receives the Zero FIR must register it and then transfer it to the police station having jurisdiction.

### Key Points:
- **No police station can refuse** to register a Zero FIR citing jurisdiction.
- Especially important in cases of **sexual assault, kidnapping, and domestic violence** where the victim may have fled to a different area.
- The Supreme Court in *Lalita Kumari v. Govt. of UP (2014)* mandated that police must register an FIR immediately upon receiving information about a cognizable offence.
- After registering the Zero FIR, the police must forward it to the jurisdictional police station within **24 hours**.

### How to File a Zero FIR:
1. Go to the nearest police station (need not be in the jurisdiction of the crime).
2. Inform them you want to file a Zero FIR.
3. Narrate the incident — they must register it.
4. Collect a copy. The FIR will be transferred to the appropriate station.

## 3. Protection of Women from Domestic Violence Act, 2005 (PWDVA)

### What Counts as Domestic Violence?
- **Physical abuse**: Hitting, slapping, kicking, burning, or any act causing bodily pain or danger to life, limb, or health.
- **Sexual abuse**: Forced sexual intercourse, forced viewing of pornography, or any sexually degrading conduct.
- **Verbal and emotional abuse**: Insults, ridicule, humiliation, name-calling, threats of causing physical pain.
- **Economic abuse**: Deprivation of financial resources, prohibition from employment, not paying rent/bills, disposing of household assets.

### Who Can File a Complaint?
- Any **woman** who is or has been in a domestic relationship (wife, live-in partner, mother, sister, widow, daughter).
- The relationship can be by **consanguinity, marriage, adoption**, or a relationship in the nature of marriage.
- The complaint can be filed against **any adult male member** of the household (husband, father-in-law, brother-in-law, etc.).

### Protections Available:
1. **Protection Order** (Section 18): Prohibits the respondent from committing domestic violence, entering the workplace/school, communicating with the aggrieved person.
2. **Residence Order** (Section 19): The woman cannot be evicted from the shared household. The respondent may be directed to arrange alternate accommodation.
3. **Monetary Relief** (Section 20): Compensation for loss of earnings, medical expenses, damage to property, and maintenance.
4. **Custody Order** (Section 21): Temporary custody of children to the aggrieved person.
5. **Compensation Order** (Section 22): Compensation for mental torture and emotional distress.

### How to Seek Help:
1. File a **Domestic Incident Report (DIR)** with a Protection Officer, police, or Service Provider.
2. The Magistrate must hear the case within **3 days** of the application and endeavour to dispose of it within **60 days**.
3. Contact the **Women Helpline: 181** for immediate assistance.

## 4. Sexual Harassment of Women at Workplace (Prevention, Prohibition and Redressal) Act, 2013

### What Constitutes Sexual Harassment?
- Physical contact and advances
- Demand or request for sexual favours
- Sexually coloured remarks
- Showing pornography
- Any other unwelcome physical, verbal, or non-verbal conduct of a sexual nature

### Key Provisions:
- Every organization with **10+ employees** must constitute an **Internal Complaints Committee (ICC)**.
- The ICC must be headed by a **woman** and have at least **one external member** from an NGO or association committed to the cause of women.
- Complaint must be filed within **3 months** of the incident (extendable by 3 months).
- The ICC must complete its inquiry within **90 days**.
- Employer must provide a **safe working environment**.

### Remedies:
- Transfer of the aggrieved woman or the respondent
- Grant of leave to the aggrieved woman
- Restraining the respondent from reporting on the woman's work performance
- Written apology, warning, reprimand, censure, or withholding of promotion
- Deduction from salary, termination, or dismissal of the respondent

### For Unorganized Sector:
- The **District Officer** constitutes a **Local Complaints Committee (LCC)**.
- Domestic workers, street vendors, agricultural labourers, and other informal workers can approach the LCC.

## 5. Dowry Prohibition Act, 1961

### Key Provisions:
- **Giving or taking dowry** is punishable with imprisonment of not less than **5 years** and a fine of not less than **Rs.15,000** or the amount of dowry, whichever is more.
- **Demanding dowry** (directly or indirectly) is punishable with imprisonment of **6 months to 2 years** and a fine up to Rs.10,000.
- Any agreement for dowry is **void**.

### Related Provisions:
- **Section 498A IPC / Section 85 BNS**: Cruelty by husband or relatives — if a woman is subjected to cruelty (including dowry harassment), the punishment is imprisonment up to **3 years** and a fine.
- **Section 304B IPC / Section 80 BNS (Dowry Death)**: If a woman dies within **7 years of marriage** under abnormal circumstances and it is shown that she was subjected to cruelty/harassment for dowry before her death, the husband/relatives face imprisonment of not less than **7 years**, extendable to life imprisonment.

### How to Report:
1. File an FIR at the nearest police station.
2. Contact the **Dowry Prohibition Officer** appointed in your district.
3. Approach the **National Commission for Women** or State Women's Commission.

| Voyeurism | 354C | 77 | 1-3 years (first), 3-7 years (repeat) |
| Stalking | 354D | 78 | Up to 3 years (first), up to 5 years (repeat) |
| Rape | 376 | 63 | 10 years to life + fine |

| Rape of minor under 16 | 376AB | 65(1) | 20 years to life |
| Rape of minor under 12 | 376AB | 65(2) | 20 years to life/death |

| Cruelty by husband/relatives | 498A | 85 | Up to 3 years + fine |

### Important Notes:
- In sexual offence cases, the **burden of proof** shifts to the accused if the victim states she did not consent.
- **Two-finger test** on rape victims has been **banned** by the Supreme Court.
- Character of the victim is **irrelevant** and inadmissible as evidence.
- A woman's statement in rape cases is sufficient to convict if it inspires confidence.

## 7. Cyber Harassment & IT Act, 2000

### Common Cyber Crimes Against Women:
- **Morphing photos** and sharing on social media
- **Cyber stalking** — repeated unwanted contact, monitoring, or tracking online
- **Revenge pornography** — sharing intimate images without consent
- **Online sexual harassment** — sexually explicit messages, comments, or emails
- **Identity theft** — creating fake profiles using someone's photos/details
- **Sextortion** — blackmailing using intimate images/videos

### Legal Provisions:
- **Section 66E IT Act**: Punishment for violation of privacy — up to 3 years + fine up to Rs.2 lakh.
- **Section 67 IT Act**: Publishing obscene material — up to 3 years + fine (first conviction), up to 5 years + fine (subsequent).
- **Section 67A IT Act**: Publishing sexually explicit material — up to 5 years + fine (first), up to 7 years + fine (subsequent).
- **Section 67B IT Act**: Publishing child sexual abuse material — up to 5 years + fine (first), up to 7 years + fine (subsequent).
- **Section 354D IPC / Section 78 BNS (Stalking)**: Applies to cyber stalking as well.

### How to Report Cyber Crimes:
1. **National Cyber Crime Reporting Portal**: cybercrime.gov.in — File complaints online.
2. **Cyber Crime Helpline**: 1930
3. **Screenshot everything** before reporting — take screenshots of messages, profiles, posts.
4. **Preserve digital evidence** — do not delete chats, emails, or posts.
5. File an FIR at the nearest **Cyber Crime Cell** or any police station.

## 8. POCSO Act, 2012 (Protection of Children from Sexual Offences)

### Key Provisions:
- Protects all children under **18 years** from sexual assault, sexual harassment, and pornography.
- Gender-neutral — protects boys and girls equally.
- **Mandatory reporting**: Anyone who knows about a POCSO offence MUST report it. Failure to report is punishable.
- **Special Courts** for speedy trial — trial must be completed within **1 year**.
- Child's statement must be recorded by a **woman officer** in the presence of a person the child trusts.
- Child's identity **cannot be disclosed** by media.

### Punishments:
- Penetrative sexual assault: Not less than **10 years**, extendable to life + fine.
- Aggravated penetrative sexual assault: Not less than **20 years**, extendable to life/death + fine.
- Sexual assault: Not less than **3 years**, extendable to 5 years + fine.
- Sexual harassment: Up to **3 years** + fine.

### Reporting:
- **Childline helpline**: 1098 (24x7, toll-free)
- Any police station — they must register an FIR under POCSO.
- The Special Juvenile Police Unit (SJPU) in each district handles POCSO cases.

## 9. Maintenance & Alimony Rights

### Who Can Claim Maintenance?
- **Wife** (including divorced wife) — under Section 125 CrPC / Section 144 BNSS, Hindu Marriage Act, Muslim Women (Protection of Rights on Divorce) Act.
- **Children** (legitimate or illegitimate) — until they attain majority; daughters until marriage.
- **Parents** — if unable to maintain themselves.

### Key Legal Provisions:
- **Section 125 CrPC / Section 144 BNSS**: Applies to all religions. A husband must maintain his wife if she is unable to maintain herself.
- **Hindu Marriage Act, Section 24**: Wife or husband can claim interim maintenance during pendency of matrimonial proceedings.
- **Hindu Adoption and Maintenance Act, Section 18**: A Hindu wife is entitled to be maintained by her husband during her lifetime.
- **Muslim Women (Protection of Rights on Divorce) Act, 1986**: A Muslim divorced woman is entitled to reasonable provision and maintenance within the iddat period, and beyond if she has not remarried.

### Amount of Maintenance:
- Courts consider: earning capacity, property, lifestyle, number of dependents, and social status.
- Generally ranges from **20% to 30%** of the husband's net income.
- **Interim maintenance** can be granted within **60 days** of the application.

### How to Claim:
1. File an application under **Section 125 CrPC** in the Magistrate's Court.
2. Or file under the relevant personal law in Family Court.
3. Legal Aid is available for women who cannot afford a lawyer.

## 10. Legal Aid & Free Legal Counsel

### Right to Free Legal Aid:
Under the **Legal Services Authorities Act, 1987**, the following are entitled to free legal aid:
- Women and children
- Members of SC/ST
- Victims of trafficking
- Persons with disabilities
- Persons in custody
- Persons with annual income below Rs.3 lakh (varies by state; up to Rs.9 lakh in some states)

### How to Access:
1. **District Legal Services Authority (DLSA)**: Visit your district court complex and approach the DLSA office.
2. **State Legal Services Authority (SLSA)**: Each state has an SLSA.
3. **National Legal Services Authority (NALSA)**: nalsa.gov.in
4. **Tele-Law**: Free legal advice via phone/video — call **14011** or visit tele-law.in
5. **Front Office** at every court complex provides free legal guidance.

### Services Provided:
- Free lawyer for court proceedings
- Legal advice and consultation
- Mediation and conciliation
- Lok Adalat for speedy settlement
- Assistance in drafting applications and complaints

## 11. National Commission for Women (NCW)

### What is NCW?
The NCW is a statutory body established under the **National Commission for Women Act, 1990** to review constitutional and legal safeguards for women, recommend remedial legislative measures, and look into complaints involving deprivation of women's rights.

### How to File a Complaint:
1. **Online**: Visit ncw.nic.in -> Register Complaint
2. **By Post**: Chairperson, National Commission for Women, Plot No. 21, FC-33, Jasola Institutional Area, New Delhi — 110025
3. **By Email**: ncw@nic.in
4. **In Person**: Visit the NCW office
5. **Helpline**: 7827-170-170

### What NCW Can Do:
- Investigate and examine matters relating to safeguards for women
- Present recommendations to the Government
- Direct police/administration to take action
- Summon and examine witnesses
- Facilitate counselling, mediation, and legal advice

### State Women's Commissions:
Each state has its own Women's Commission. Complaints can also be filed with the state commission for faster local action.

## 13. Step-by-Step: What to Do in an Emergency

### If You Are in Immediate Physical Danger:
1. **Call 112** (National Emergency Number) immediately.
2. Try to move to a **public place** or a neighbor's house.
3. If possible, **send your live location** to a trusted contact.
4. Do NOT confront the aggressor if you feel it may escalate the situation.

### If You Are a Victim of Domestic Violence:
1. **Call 181** (Women Helpline) or **112** for immediate help.
2. **Document everything**: Take photos of injuries, screenshot threatening messages, note dates and witnesses.
3. Visit the nearest **police station** and file an FIR.
4. Contact a **Protection Officer** through the Women Helpline or District Magistrate's office.
5. Apply for a **Protection Order** in the Magistrate's Court.
6. Visit a **One Stop Centre (Sakhi Centre)** in your district for comprehensive support.

### If You Are Sexually Assaulted:
1. **Do not bathe, change clothes, or wash** — preserve physical evidence.
2. **Call 112** and go to the nearest **hospital** for a medical examination.
3. Under **Section 357C CrPC**, all hospitals (government and private) MUST provide **free first aid** to sexual assault victims. Refusal is punishable.
4. File an **FIR** — the police MUST register it. If they refuse, approach the SP or file a Zero FIR at any other station.
5. Your statement will be recorded by a **female officer**. You can have a person of your choice present.
6. The **medical examination** must be conducted by a female doctor. Your consent is required for every step.
7. You are entitled to **free legal aid** — contact the DLSA.

### If You Are Being Harassed Online:
1. **Screenshot and save** all evidence (messages, posts, profiles, emails).
2. **Do not delete** any digital communication.
3. Report on the **National Cyber Crime Portal**: cybercrime.gov.in or call **1930**.
4. **Report and block** the person on the social media platform.
5. File an **FIR** at the nearest police station or cyber crime cell.

### If You Are Facing Workplace Sexual Harassment:
1. **Document everything** — emails, messages, witness names, dates.
2. File a **written complaint** with the **Internal Complaints Committee (ICC)** within 3 months.
3. If no ICC exists (or the employer is the harasser), approach the **Local Complaints Committee (LCC)** at the District Officer's office.
4. You can simultaneously file an **FIR** under Section 354 IPC / Section 74 BNS.
5. The employer **cannot terminate or transfer** you for filing a complaint (anti-retaliation provision).

### If You Need Immediate Legal Advice:
1. Call **14011** (Tele-Law) for free telephonic legal consultation.
2. Visit the **District Legal Services Authority (DLSA)** at your district court complex.
3. Contact the **NCW Helpline**: 7827-170-170.
4. Visit a **One Stop Centre (Sakhi Centre)** — they provide free legal aid.

## Important Constitutional Rights

- **Article 14**: Right to Equality — equal protection of law.
- **Article 15(3)**: State can make special provisions for women and children.
- **Article 21**: Right to Life and Personal Liberty — includes right to live with dignity, right to privacy.
- **Article 39(a)**: Right to adequate means of livelihood.
- **Article 39(d)**: Equal pay for equal work.
- **Article 42**: Just and humane conditions of work and maternity relief.
- **Article 51A(e)**: Fundamental Duty to renounce practices derogatory to the dignity of women.

## Recent Supreme Court Landmark Judgments

1. **Vishakha v. State of Rajasthan (1997)**: Laid down guidelines for prevention of sexual harassment at workplace (precursor to the 2013 Act).
2. **Lalita Kumari v. Govt. of UP (2014)**: Mandatory registration of FIR for cognizable offences — police cannot refuse.
3. **Joseph Shine v. Union of India (2018)**: Struck down Section 497 (adultery as crime by man) — declared unconstitutional and discriminatory to women.
4. **Independent Thought v. Union of India (2017)**: Criminalized sexual intercourse with wife under 18 (marital rape exception for minor wives removed).
5. **Shayara Bano v. Union of India (2017)**: Triple talaq declared unconstitutional.
6. **X v. Principal Secretary (2023)**: Married women's right to abortion under MTP Act — marital rape recognized as ground for abortion.
